🧰 Common Dental Instruments

Dental instruments are designed for examination, restorative, periodontal, endodontic and surgical tasks. Learning their names and general purposes helps students become familiar with the clinical environment.

InstrumentGeneral purpose
Mouth mirrorProvides indirect vision and can help with soft-tissue retraction.
Explorer / probeAssists with examination of tooth surfaces and clinical features.
College tweezersCarry and place small items such as cotton pellets.
Spoon excavatorUsed in selected restorative procedures to remove soft carious dentin.
ScalerDesigned for removal of calculus during periodontal instrumentation.
Dental forcepsUsed for tooth extraction according to tooth anatomy and the clinical situation.
Learning point: Instrument choice and use depend on the clinical task, instrument design and the operator's training. This table is an introduction, not a clinical procedure manual.
Instrument basics

🔩 Understanding Dental Burs

Dental burs are rotary instruments available in different shapes, sizes and cutting designs. Selection depends on the procedure, tooth structure, material and compatible handpiece.

Round burs

Have a spherical cutting head. Certain designs may be used in selected cavity preparation tasks.

Fissure burs

Have an elongated cutting surface and are available in different designs.

Tapered burs

Narrow toward one end and have geometry suited to particular preparation designs.

Why shape matters

Bur dimensions, cutting design, access and the intended task all influence selection.

Clinical safety: Rotary instruments can injure teeth and soft tissues if used incorrectly. Use requires appropriate training, supervision and protective measures.

🔢 FDI Tooth Numbering System

The FDI two-digit notation system identifies teeth using a quadrant digit followed by a tooth-position digit. It is commonly used in dental records and communication.

Permanent dentition quadrants

First digitQuadrant
1Upper right
2Upper left
3Lower left
4Lower right

The second digit runs from 1 for the central incisor to 8 for the third molar within each permanent quadrant.

Tooth 11

Maxillary right central incisor.

Tooth 36

Mandibular left first molar.

Tooth 44

Mandibular right first premolar.

Memory aid

First digit = quadrant; second digit = tooth position.

Endodontic overview

🩺 Root Canal Treatment (RCT) Basics

Root canal treatment manages disease or injury involving the dental pulp and root canal system. The treatment plan depends on diagnosis, tooth anatomy and individual clinical findings.

  1. Assessment and diagnosis: Review relevant history, examination findings and diagnostic investigations.
  2. Isolation: Establish an appropriate working environment and control moisture.
  3. Access: Prepare access to the pulp chamber and canal system.
  4. Cleaning and shaping: Prepare the canal system using a suitable technique and instrument system.
  5. Irrigation: Use appropriate irrigating solutions as part of canal cleaning, with attention to safe use.
  6. Obturation: Fill the prepared canal system using suitable materials when indicated.
  7. Final restoration: Restore the tooth based on the remaining structure and clinical assessment.
Remember: This is a simplified learning overview. It does not replace clinical diagnosis, supervised endodontic training or patient-specific treatment planning.
Essential vocabulary

📚 Dental Terms for Beginners

Clinical examination

Assessment using relevant patient history and appropriate examination methods.

Diagnosis

Identification of a condition based on clinical findings and diagnostic information.

Restoration

A treatment used to restore the form or function of a tooth affected by caries, fracture, wear or other damage.

Endodontics

The branch of dentistry concerned with the dental pulp and tissues surrounding tooth roots.

Periodontics

The branch of dentistry concerned with the supporting tissues of teeth, including gingiva, periodontal ligament and alveolar bone.
